Voice Computer Agent

Tell your Mac what to do. It does it on screen while you watch, or in the background while you sleep.

Not released yet. It is being tested on one Mac, and the download goes here when it is ready.

What it does

You hold a key and say what you want, such as “get me to my Chrome extensions” or “what should I do next on this page”. You can also type the goal into a box. The agent looks at your screen, picks one step, does it, looks again, and repeats until the job is finished.

It can point while you click, click for you while you watch, or work in the background inside limits you set before bed. A very faint green glow around the edge of the screen shows it is on. Press Escape and it stops at once.

How it works

The whole agent is one function called again and again: next_action(goal, screen). It takes your goal and the current screen and returns exactly one action, such as “click here”, “type this” or “done”.

1. You give a goal

In: your voice while you hold Right Option, or text in the goal box. Out: one line of text. Speech becomes text on your Mac with whisper.cpp. The microphone is only on while you hold the key.

2. It looks at the screen

In: the screen. Out: one small screenshot, kept in memory and never saved, plus the names of the buttons and fields macOS reports. Text on the screen is treated as data only. A web page that says “ignore your user” gets no say.

3. It picks one action

In: the goal and the screen. Out: one action in a strict format. Any reply that is not exactly one valid action is thrown away. Free mode asks a model on your Mac. Claude mode asks Claude.

4. Safety check

In: the action and what is on screen. Out: go, ask first, or blocked. Sending, deleting and settings changes need your yes. Password fields, payment fields, password managers, Keychain, System Settings and banking sites are always off limits.

5. It acts

In: an approved action. Out: a mouse click, keystrokes or a scroll. In guide mode it only draws a ring around the spot, and the up and down arrow keys step through the plan. In background mode it presses buttons inside one app without moving your mouse.

6. It remembers what worked

When a task succeeds, its steps are saved. Next time it replays them with no AI calls. Before each replayed step it checks that the screen still matches. Anything you typed is never saved.

Three ways to use it

Guide: it points, you click

A ring shows what to click next. Up and down arrows step forward and back. Nothing is clicked for you.

Auto: it clicks while you watch

It moves the mouse and types on your screen. A faint green glow sits around the screen edge the whole time.

Overnight: full control inside hard limits

Before bed you list the jobs and set the limits: hours, AI tokens and steps. You type GRANT to confirm. It works through the list, in the background inside one app if you choose. While it works in the background, the green glow slowly breathes and a small label at the top says “Working in background” with the app’s name. Anything that needs your yes, such as sending or deleting, waits in a list for the morning unless you allowed it.

vca grant --goal "sort my Downloads folder into folders" --goal "draft replies to unread mail" --hours 8 --max-tokens 200000 --app Mail
vca overnight
vca held      # in the morning: what waited for you
vca revoke    # cancel the grant at any time

These limits hold in every mode and no setting can turn them off: it never spends money (no buying, paying, checkout, subscribing, donating, transfers or adding cards), never types into password or payment fields, never touches password managers, Keychain, System Settings or banking sites, and never presses Escape or changes its own settings. A grant lasts at most 12 hours.

Other AI companies can plug in through the same provider interface: one class with one method.

Connecting it to Claude

  1. 1
    Get an API keySign in at console.anthropic.com, open API Keys, and create a key.
  2. 2
    Store it in your KeychainPaste this into Terminal. It asks for the key, which never goes into a file or a log: security add-generic-password -s voice-computer-agent-anthropic -a me -w
  3. 3
    Say yes onceThe first Claude-mode run explains what is sent and asks for your consent. Free mode never asks, because nothing leaves your Mac.
  4. 4
    Run itvca run --provider claude --mode auto --goal "get me to my Chrome extensions"

How to install (once released)

  1. 1
    Download and unzipThe download button appears at the top of this page at release.
  2. 2
    Install the free AIInstall Ollama from ollama.com, then run ollama pull qwen3.5:9b (about 8 GB).
  3. 3
    Allow two permissionsIn System Settings, Privacy & Security, turn on Accessibility and Screen Recording for the app. It asks for nothing else.
  4. 4
    Check itRun vca doctor. It lists anything still missing.
  5. 5
    GoHold Right Option and speak, or run vca run --box to type a goal.
